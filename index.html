    AI Advertising Platform

    Executive Summary

    A web platform, built in-house, that runs Facebook and Instagram advertising for product brands from one place: the product catalogue, a picture library, AI-generated ad pictures in the brand's own styles, a post composer, and campaign set-up with spending limits and approvals. The interface works in English and Georgian.

    The Challenge

    Small marketing teams spend most of their week on repetitive production: product photos, price banners, the same post in several formats, and a manual check before anything is paid for. General AI image tools help little in Georgia: they do not render Georgian script reliably and they ignore a brand's visual rules.

    Technical Solution

    Products are imported from the shop's own Excel or CSV stock file (name, quantity, price in GEL); every row is checked and skipped rows are listed by their row number. Pictures live in a library with folders, tags and automatic clean-up of archived pictures after 30 days. The brand's picture styles are rules and options the team edits in the browser, imported from its prompt library in Excel. The composer assembles the prompt, sends the product photo and chosen style pictures to Google's Gemini image models, then draws the Georgian product name onto the picture itself, so the text is always exact. Campaigns are set up in a five-step wizard with budget limits, a typed confirmation and a log of every change, validated against Meta's Marketing API in dry-run mode before money is spent. Access is members-only and enforced in the database.

    Business Impact

    Products, pictures and campaigns sit in one searchable place. Georgian text on ad pictures is always correct. Nothing is published or spent without an explicit approval, and every change leaves a record. Built-in limits (30 AI pictures a day, 50 MB of library storage per person) keep running costs predictable.

    Technology Stack

    Google Gemini image models, Meta Marketing API, Supabase (PostgreSQL, Auth, Storage, Edge Functions), row-level security, SheetJS, vanilla JavaScript.

    Tender Analytics Platform

    Executive Summary

    A web platform for companies that bid in Georgian public procurement. It collects every tender in four categories (furniture, food, hygiene, construction materials) from the state procurement system, reads all of its documents, and turns them into requirements, deadlines, typical prices and competitor histories. It is built as a paid service: data is shown only after sign-in and is kept in private storage.

    The Challenge

    To find the few tenders worth bidding on, and the price that wins, a bidder has to read each tender's technical specification, price tables and contract draft, often several PDFs and spreadsheets per tender. Past winning prices are scattered across thousands of files. Doing this by hand for hundreds of tenders a month is not possible.

    Technical Solution

    A resumable collector downloads tenders, bids, contracts and documents in small overnight batches. A document reader (PDF, Word, Excel) extracts each requirement, tags it (standards, certificates, laboratory tests), rates the tender's complexity and finds similar past tenders. Bidders' own price tables are read line by line and checked against the bid total. The site offers a dashboard, a tender list with filters, a page per tender, company and buyer pages with contract timelines, and a Products page with every bidder's line prices. Sign-in with Google; the data sits in private cloud storage.

    Business Impact

    900+ tenders, with the documents of 920 of them read (3,827 files, 28,642 requirement items, no failures); 442 companies and 517 buyers profiled. Checked against an expert's own labelling, the reader found 77 of 79 requirements in full and the other 2 in part. Product prices: 855 bidders' files from the last three months, 408 price tables read, 4,084 offer lines; 305 tables (75%) add up to the bid total.

    Technology Stack

    Python collectors and document readers (PDF, Word, Excel), Supabase Auth and private Storage, Google sign-in, statically generated pages.

    AI CV Reading & Job Matching

    Executive Summary

    Personal Career Strategist is an online career service. The client signs in with Google, LinkedIn or email, uploads a CV and answers a preferences questionnaire. The platform reads the CV with AI, finds and scores vacancies, and prepares applications that are sent only after the client ticks them. A client dashboard and an admin panel with live chat run the service day to day.

    The Challenge

    Job seekers lose hours every week scanning job boards and rewriting the same CV, and many applications go to roles that never fitted. A service that does this for them has to be quick, show why a vacancy fits, and handle personal data with care.

    Technical Solution

    CV reading: the text is taken out of the PDF in the browser and the name and contact details are removed first; only then does Claude (Anthropic) sort the rest into experience, education, skills and languages, and the client can turn it into a one-page CV (PDF or JPG). Matching: every vacancy gets a 0–100 score from six weighted factors (job title 45, skills 20, seniority 15, location 10, salary 5, language 5). The dashboard lists companies first with their matched vacancies, deadlines and status; applications go out only for vacancies the client approved. The admin panel shows every client's plan, searches and profile completeness, with live chat.

    Business Impact

    The service page shows the live funnel from vacancies found to applications sent. Personal data never reaches the AI model with the client's name or contacts attached, and no application goes out without the client's own approval.

    Technology Stack

    Claude API (Anthropic), Supabase (Auth, PostgreSQL, Storage, Edge Functions), Google and LinkedIn sign-in, Python pipeline, Flitt hosted checkout, vanilla JavaScript.

    Amazon Listing Monitor

    Executive Summary

    A read-only monitoring pipeline for a German publisher of gifts and stationery that sells on amazon.de. Every day it pulls what Amazon currently shows for each product, stores a snapshot, logs every change, and writes the result into the team's Google Sheet, with an email summary or alert. It never changes anything on Amazon.

    The Challenge

    Listing content on Amazon can change without notice: titles, bullet points, images, A+ content and variations are altered by catalogue updates. The seller's team had no way to notice, and checking by hand in Seller Central was not repeatable for about 1,000 products.

    Technical Solution

    Python on an existing Azure server, with a SQLite database that keeps daily snapshots and a permanent change log. Amazon's Selling Partner API supplies catalogue data, A+ content, seller reports and image dates; requests go in small batches to stay within Amazon's limits and resume automatically after a failure. A self-hosted n8n workflow runs the check every day at 18:00 Berlin time, writes a per-product tab and a per-SKU tab into the Google Sheet with the changes marked, and sends the email summary or alert.

    Business Impact

    935 products (1,873 SKUs) are checked in about 40 minutes per run, with all 935 completed and no failures in the last five recorded runs. A+ content is tracked on 780 of the 935 products. The team sees exactly what changed and when, at no running cost beyond the existing Azure server and Google Workspace.

    Technology Stack

    Python, Amazon Selling Partner API, SQLite, self-hosted n8n, Google Sheets, Azure.

    MCG Market: Motorcycle Marketplace

    Executive Summary

    MCG Market (mclub.ge) is a Georgian online marketplace for motorcycles, motorcycle parts and riding gear. It grew out of a Facebook community of about 70,000 members. Our team designed and built the platform.

    The Challenge

    Buying and selling motorcycles in Georgia happened in a large Facebook group: posts sank in the feed, nothing could be filtered by brand, year or engine size, and parts and gear were mixed in with bikes. The community needed one place that is easy to search and easy to post in.

    Technical Solution

    A marketplace in three categories (motorcycles, parts, gear) with search and filters by make, model, location, condition, year, engine size and price, and a brand filter for 10 makes. Sellers sign in and post free listings with photos and details in five steps; buyers message sellers through the site. A shops directory, a blog with news and guides, a customs-duty calculator for imported motorcycles (by model year and engine size) and a contact form complete it. Built with Next.js on Vercel, with images in cloud storage. The platform takes no payments: buyers and sellers deal directly.

    Business Impact

    The community's trade moved from a social feed to a structured catalogue: every listing carries details the filters can search, and buyers reach sellers without leaving the platform. The customs calculator answers the import-duty question before a purchase.

    Technology Stack

    Next.js, Vercel, Vercel Blob storage, on-site messaging, search and filters, Georgian interface.
